02

The Work, Unbundled.

These aren't ten products — they're the concrete work one router and the team behind it can do. Hire us for one, or for several.

01

L7 router deployment into your core.

A layer-7 router or reverse proxy installed and configured at the center of an existing network — services mapped as backends, the routing table defined around real traffic, access rules established. What distinguishes it is not the router but the accountability held by those who built it. Measured in days.

→
02

Incident response.

When a system fails, an engineer who already knows it is present within minutes and acts while the incident is in progress — rerouting around the failing node, isolating the segment, gracefully degrading the non-essential so the critical path keeps running. The speed follows from already being inside.

→
03

Readiness, maintained.

The continuous work that precedes any incident: a current map of the infrastructure and a response plan kept aligned with the system as it changes. The most expensive part of an outage — reconstructing an unfamiliar environment under pressure — is moved out of the crisis and into calm, so response begins from knowledge rather than from scratch.

→
04

Degradation monitoring.

Observation of traffic at the routing layer, where the early indications of failure — rising latency, emerging errors, a faltering integration — appear before users feel them. A category of incident is resolved on approach and never becomes an outage at all.

→
05

Infrastructure & software work.

Correction, extension, and preparation of a system according to what it objectively requires — a fault repaired, functionality added, or capacity readied for growth. The course followed is the one the infrastructure actually needs, even where that means building less rather than more, never what deepens dependence on the provider.

→
06

Peak-load preparation.

Ahead of a season or a launch, a system is put under realistic load, its weak points located and reinforced — so a surge does not cause failure at the moment of greatest consequence. The failure point is raised; certainty is not promised.

→
07

Abuse protection.

Floods, bad bots, and abuse aimed at a set of services are filtered, rate-limited, and blocked at the routing layer, with human intervention where automated defenses prove insufficient. The scope is stated plainly: protection within the bounds of an organization's own infrastructure, not a globally distributed absorption network.

→
08

Access management.

Roles, routes, and permissions configured so that traffic reaches its intended destination with the intended level of access — and kept current as circumstances change, rather than drifting back into disorder. Access at the routing layer; a complement to existing identity and authentication systems, not a replacement.

→
09

Cost audit.

What actually flows through an infrastructure — including runaway automated and AI traffic — rendered visible from the point through which it passes, with cost and load anomalies identified before they run for months. Observation and a control point on traffic, not a full financial-operations platform.

→
10

On-prem delivery of our software.

For an organization with the competence and the preference to run the router itself: the software provided for deployment on its own infrastructure, with setup assistance and documentation. No retainer and no dependence on the provider — the tool, operated under the organization's own control.
